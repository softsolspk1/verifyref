HOW TO READ THE FIGURES
- "Test set" results use references or manuscripts with known correct answers (ground truth).
- "Benchmark" results use published datasets labelled by experts (SciFact, BioFors, retracted
  paper-mill papers). Benchmarks are harder and closer to real use than our own test sets.
- A perfect score on a small set does not mean zero errors. With 0 errors in N cases the true
  error rate is below about 3/N with 95% confidence (85 cases -> below about 3.5%).

Fixed on 5 Nov (found by the feature test):
- .bib files with entries on one line (Google Scholar, Zotero) were read as empty. Fixed.
- References run together on one line ([1] ... [2] ..., or unnumbered) were read as one. Fixed.
- A DOI with the page numbers glued on ("10.1038/nmeth.2089671-675") was reported as a
  non-existent DOI. It is now repaired and the reference verified.
- A reference whose title resembled a different work (other authors, pages and source) was
  called Miscited. It is now reported as not found, with that work as the closest record.
- References to predatory journals could be missed: the journal of a wrongly matched record
  was checked instead of the cited journal, and in the first seconds after a server start the
  predatory and retraction lists were still loading. Both fixed (checks wait for the lists).

Honest limits:
- True error rate: below about 3.5% (95% confidence). 200+ references from real PJPS submissions
  would be needed to show an error rate below 1-2%.
- Regional Pakistani journals that no database indexes are reported "Unverified / Not indexed",
  not confirmed. Books, theses and guidelines usually end as "Unverified - check manually".
- Results depend on the online databases answering; when several do not, the reference is left
  Unverified rather than called fabricated.

3B. Does the cited source support the claim?

- Real PDF and open full text, 6 claims with known answers: 6 / 6 correct, with page numbers
- Generated sources, 7 claims (supported, opposite, wrong figure, no source, retracted
  evidence, invented work): 7 / 7

SciFact benchmark (1,259 expert-labelled claim-source pairs, ABSTRACT ONLY), version of 5 Nov
(language model with the rule refitted on the train split; figures on the held-out split):

                                                   Before (4 Nov)   Now (5 Nov)
Supported claims found (Full or Partial)           73%              72%
Supported claims judged "Full support"             28%              22%
Contradicted claims flagged as contradicted        27%              38%
Contradicted claims passed as "Full support"       1%               1%
No-evidence claims passed as "Full support"        2.3%             0.8%
Supported claims wrongly called contradicted       7%               7%

Fixed on 5 Nov: the citation inside a claim ("... (Vaswani et al., 2017).") made the language
model reject a source stating the claim word for word; and "our model achieves ..." in a source
was not linked to the name the claim uses. Both fixed (real PDF test now 6 / 6).

Meaning: a "Full support" verdict can be trusted (almost never given to a wrong claim), but
about 1 in 4 supported claims is missed and about 6 in 10 contradictions are not flagged when
only the abstract is available. With the uploaded PDF or open full text, more support is found.

A larger language model (DeBERTa-v3-base) was tested the same way and did not do better on the
held-out claims, so the smaller, faster model stays.

3C. Citation patterns (self-citation, stacking, author concentration, off-topic, blocks)
- 6 of 6 planted patterns flagged. Small test only; no public benchmark exists.


=====================================================================================
MODULE 4 - PAPER MILL DETECTION
=====================================================================================

Planted test cases:
- Clean manuscript: LOW (score 0). Planted mill manuscript: HIGH (score 28).
- 10 of 10 planted signals found; tortured phrases 39 of 40 with 0 false alarms.
- Exact and lightly edited resubmissions, and author changes between versions: detected.

Benchmark: 150 real papers retracted for paper-mill reasons against 126 ordinary papers from
the same journals and years ("flagged" = MEDIUM or HIGH risk):

Signals                                         Mill papers flagged   Ordinary papers flagged
----------------------------------------------  --------------------  -----------------------
Text signals only                               24 / 150 (16%)        2 / 126 (2%)
Text + look-alikes of known mill papers         38 / 150 (25%)        3 / 126 (2%)
Flagged HIGH (text signals)                     3 / 150               0 / 126

Meaning: about 3 in 4 real mill papers are NOT flagged from the text alone. False alarms are
low (2%). If 2% of submissions come from mills, only about 1 in 6 flagged manuscripts would
really be a mill paper: a flag means "look closer", not proof.

Honest limits:
- The look-alike signal finds new papers from mills already caught; it does not find a
  brand-new mill.
- Cover letter, submission waves, suggested reviewers, ORCID and authorship changes could only
  be tested with planted cases, not on real mill papers.
