PJPS DATA INTEGRITY SYSTEM - MODULE 3B AND 3C: GAPS FIXED ON 8 NOVEMBER 2026
Prepared by Softsols Pakistan

All numbers below were measured on the 39 Full Screening / Module 3 reports saved on the live system
(25 different manuscripts), not on made-up examples, unless marked "planted".


=====================================================================================
1. MODULE 3B - CLAIM SUPPORTED BY THE CITED SOURCE
=====================================================================================

What the saved reports showed
  52 claim-source checks in the saved reports:
    12 (23%) were read from a full text
    40 (77%) were read from the ABSTRACT ONLY
  Of the 40 abstract-only checks, 26 came out "Unsupported". A claim that is not in the abstract
  can still be in the paper itself, so most of these are "not shown", not "wrong".

Why the abstract only
  The cited papers were checked one by one (33 different papers). 31 of them are behind a
  paywall (Elsevier, ACS, Wiley, Taylor & Francis ...): no legal free full text exists. The
  system already tries 7 open sources before the abstract (Europe PMC, Unpaywall, OpenAlex,
  CORE, Semantic Scholar, bioRxiv/medRxiv, arXiv).

Fix 1 - PubMed Central author manuscripts
  Papers that are free to read in PubMed Central but are not in its open-licence subset (for
  example author manuscripts of funded research) were skipped. They are now read through the
  official NCBI E-utilities service. On the 33 papers: 2 more now get a full text (Chem. Rev.
  2017 "Chalcone: a privileged structure"; Allergy 2025 GLP-1R/GIPR signalling).
  (PMC's web pages answer automated requests with a CAPTCHA, so they are not used.)

Fix 2 - References without a DOI or PMID
  Older papers and local journals often have no DOI. Their source text is now looked up in
  Europe PMC by exact title (accepted only when the titles agree at least 90% both ways).

Fix 3 - The editor is told which PDFs to upload
  Uploading the cited PDF is the only way to check a claim against a paywalled paper, and the
  system already reads uploaded PDFs page by page. Each Module 3B result now says where the
  sources were read from and lists the references to upload, for example:
    "Cited sources read: 3 from open full texts, 19 from the abstract only. To settle the
     verdicts read from the abstract or with no source text, upload the PDFs of references
     [4], [7], [12] ... and run the check again."
  The same lines are in the Word export.

Honest limit
  On the SciFact expert benchmark (abstracts only) the measured accuracy is unchanged: 72% of
  supported claims found, 38% of contradictions flagged, 1% of contradicted claims wrongly
  passed. Larger language models were tested on 5 November and did not do better. The real
  gain for PJPS comes from full texts: open ones (now a little more) and uploaded PDFs.


=====================================================================================
2. MODULE 3C - CITATION PATTERNS
=====================================================================================

What the saved reports showed (25 different manuscripts)
  "Off-topic references" was raised on 14 of 25 (56%). Almost all were false alarms, for
  example "Programmed cell death in osteoarthritis" called off-topic in an osteoarthritis
  paper. Causes:
    - words were weighted by how rare they are in the reference list itself, so the topic
      words that most references share weighed almost nothing;
    - only the title / abstract was compared, so methods sources (SwissADME, Gaussian, the ICH
      guideline, histology manuals) looked unrelated;
    - badly split reference entries ("Mol", "(A) Representative fluorescence images", a run of
      author names, "FOR PEER REVIEW ...") were judged as if they were titles.
  "Author concentration" was raised 3 times for one research group (once per co-author).
  In one report the heading "META-ANALYSIS" was taken as a manuscript author.

Fixes
  1. Relevance is now judged against the WHOLE manuscript text: a reference is weak only when
     fewer than a quarter of its title words occur anywhere in the paper AND it shares no
     subject word with the title, abstract or keywords.
  2. Entries that are not real titles (too short, author lists, captions, publisher details)
     are "not judged" instead of "off-topic".
  3. The title of a matched record is used only when the reference was verified as that paper.
  4. Co-authors of the same papers are one research group and one flag.
  5. Self-citation:
     - two different ORCID iDs are two different people, whatever the names;
     - a very common surname alone (Khan, Ali, Ahmad, Wang, Li, Zhang, Kumar ...) is not
       enough - a full or initialled name, or the ORCID, is needed;
     - title words ("META-ANALYSIS", "Systematic Review") are not taken as author names.
  6. Off-topic flag: with the manuscript text, 2 or more weak references (high when 5 or more
     and 15% of the list); the flag now lists the references. Without the text the old rule
     (20% of the list) stays.

Results
                                               Before        After
  Real manuscripts flagged "off-topic"         14 of 25      0 of 25
  Author-concentration flags (same 25)         5             3   (same groups, one flag each)
  Planted test: 3 unrelated references added
    to each real manuscript - flagged          0 of 25       23 of 25
  Planted test: 5 unrelated references added   2 of 25       25 of 25
  Planted audit (test/audit-m3b.js)            7/7 flags     7/7 flags

New test: test/citation-pattern.js (part of "npm test"): padded references found, clean list
not flagged, debris not judged, one flag per research group, self-citation not by a common
surname alone or across different ORCID iDs, "META-ANALYSIS" not an author.
